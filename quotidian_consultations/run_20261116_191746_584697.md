## 2. Token assumptions per article

- 6,000 visible characters ÷ ~4 characters per token (typical for English) ≈ **1,500 tokens**.
- Dataset context (definition, e.g. "seizures of product X", plus the label options such as evidence, not evidence, other) ≈ **300 tokens**, assuming it is sent with every article.
- **Total ≈ 1,800 input tokens per article.**
- Output tokens: the label or score is free, so they add $0.

If you feed raw HTML instead of visible text, the token count could be several times higher, so strip markup first.

## 5. Other relevant metrics

- **Multiple datasets:** if each article is scored against N datasets and you resend the full article each time, cost scales linearly. Ten datasets would cost about **$16/month**, and 50 datasets about **$82/month**. If JEV can score several labels or datasets in one call, the cost stays near the base case.
- **Cost per relevant article:** if, say, 5% of articles qualify as evidence (about 1,083 per month), the classification cost is about **$0.0015 per evidence item**.
- **Throughput:** 5,000 articles per week is about 0.5 articles per minute on average, so rate limits are very unlikely to matter. Check latency per call if you batch.
- **Hidden costs:** the real costs are likely elsewhere, such as crawling or monitoring feeds, text extraction and deduplication, storage, and human review of borderline cases. Review time will far exceed the roughly $1.64 inference bill.
- **Savings:** deduplicating syndicated or wire stories before classification can cut volume by 20–40% in news monitoring, though at this price the savings are only cents.

## Bottom line

Classifying 5,000 articles per week with JEV should cost about **$0.38 per week, or roughly $1.50–$2.00 per month** (about $20 per year). Even at 10× the volume or with many datasets, it stays in the low tens of dollars per month.
