## 5. Other relevant metrics

- **Multiple datasets:** cost scales linearly with the number of datasets each article is checked against. Ten datasets (for example seizures of different products) would cost about **$18/month** in the base case.
- **Cost per relevant hit:** if about 5% of articles are true evidence (~1,083/month), the cost is ≈ **$0.0017 per evidence article found** in the base case. This figure is illustrative, since your hit rate will differ.
- **Break-even against an LLM:** a typical small LLM priced at, say, $0.15/M input and $0.60/M output would cost about $13–20/month for the same job, roughly 7–10× more. This depends on the model and its current pricing, and it needs a short reasoning output, so treat it as a rough comparison.
- **Hidden costs outside JEV:** these are usually larger than the JEV bill. They include scraping and monitoring infrastructure, text extraction and cleaning, storage, and human review of borderline cases.
- **Cost reduction levers:**
  - Strip boilerplate such as navigation, ads, and comments, which can cut input by 20–40%.
  - Truncate to the first N tokens when the lead paragraphs are enough.
  - Deduplicate syndicated articles.
  - Pre-filter with cheap keyword rules.

## Bottom line

At 5,000 articles/week, JEV costs about **$1.40–$1.80 per month**, with a realistic ceiling around **$2.30**. That is under **$0.0001 per article**, so the decision cost is negligible next to the rest of the pipeline. To tighten the estimate, run a sample of 100 articles through JEV and check the actual token counts it reports.
